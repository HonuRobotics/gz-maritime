Put rain on a camera’s lens

A camera on a small boat rides close to the water, and in rain or spray its lens is rarely clear. The gz_lens_drops package puts drops of water on a camera sensor’s images. Each drop is a small lens: it shows a wide, upside-down view of the scene, darkens at its rim and blurs, as a real drop does.

The drops also behave like water on glass:

  • They land where the rain moves into the lens, so a forward camera catches more at speed or into the wind.

  • They stay put until they are big enough to slide, then slide down the world, following the boat’s roll and pitch.

  • They merge, and leave beads and wet trails behind them.

  • They dry from the top, keeping their outline, as water on glass does.

It works with a plain camera sensor and the ogre2 render engine, and it needs a GPU, as every camera does. Only the sensor’s images show the drops; the Gazebo GUI’s view stays clear.

Add it to a camera

Put the plugin inside the camera’s <sensor>, next to <camera>. With the camera of Add sensors:

<sensor name="camera" type="camera">
  <frame_id>${name}/camera_link</frame_id>
  <topic>${name}/camera/image</topic>
  <update_rate>15</update_rate>
  <always_on>true</always_on>
  <camera>
    <horizontal_fov>1.2</horizontal_fov>
    <image><width>640</width><height>480</height></image>
    <clip><near>0.05</near><far>200</far></clip>
  </camera>
  <plugin filename="gz-maritime-lens-drops-system"
          name="gz::sim::maritime::LensDrops">
    <rain_rate>10</rain_rate>
    <focus>0.5</focus>
  </plugin>
</sensor>

Element

Default

What

<rain_rate>

0

Rain, in mm/h: 2 is light rain, 10 moderate, 50 a downpour.

<exposure>

1

How open the lens is to the rain, from 0 behind a deep hood to 1.

<focus>

0.5

0 for drops on the lens itself, out of focus; 1 for drops on a window some way ahead, sharp.

<dry_time>

20

Seconds a drop lasts.

<contact_angle>

0.96

How water sits on the lens, in radians, up to pi/2; raise it for a water-repellent coating, which beads water.

<slide_radius>

0.03

How big a drop must be, in image heights, before gravity alone moves it; lower it for a water-repellent coating, which sheds water sooner.

<wind>

true

Use the world’s wind for the rain’s drift and the push on the drops.

<mask>

false

Publish a mask of where the drops are.

<enabled>

true

Draw the drops; false keeps them but leaves the image clear.

<seed>

1

The same seed gives the same drops on every run; 0 draws a new one and logs it.

The package README lists one more element, <map_scale>, and the ranges of every value.

The plugin is installed with the workspace, and the workspace’s setup script tells Gazebo where to find it.

How much rain lands

Rain falls at about 6.5 m/s. What reaches the lens is the part of that motion, with the wind’s drift and the boat’s own motion, that goes into the lens:

  • A camera facing up gets all of it.

  • A level camera on a still boat in calm air gets almost none; a little comes from splashes off its housing.

  • The same camera on a boat at 5 m/s gets about three quarters as much as one facing up, and more into a headwind.

So the same <rain_rate> gives a dry lens in the harbour and a wet one at speed. The state topic below reports this as flux.

Change the rain while it runs

Each camera with the plugin takes changes on its own topic, named after the sensor:

gz topic -t /world/default/model/my_boat/link/camera_sensor/sensor/camera/lens_drops/set \
  -m gz.msgs.Param \
  -p 'params { key: "rain_rate" value { type: DOUBLE double_value: 40 } }'

  • Keys: enabled, rain_rate, exposure, focus, dry_time, contact_angle, slide_radius and clear. A non-zero clear wipes the lens, as a wiper would.

  • One drop: add_x, add_y and add_radius together put one drop on the lens, which is handy for testing a perception pipeline.

  • From ROS: bridge the topic to ros_gz_interfaces/msg/ParamVec.

Five times a second of simulation time, the plugin reports the lens on .../lens_drops/state:

  • the values;

  • count and sliding, the drops on the lens and how many slide;

  • covered, the share of the image with water;

  • flux and the gravity along the lens;

  • draws, how many times the drops were drawn;

  • attached, which turns true once the render pass is on the camera (a shader that fails is in the log), and drawing, whether it drew the drops in the last frame.

If attached stays false while the camera publishes images, the server’s log says why, under [LensDrops].

Ground truth for perception

With <mask>true</mask>, the plugin also publishes .../lens_drops/mask. It is a grey image at the drop map’s size (half the camera’s resolution with the default <map_scale>), 255 where a drop is, published with each camera frame while someone subscribes and stamped as that frame. Use it to train or score a raindrop detector against the camera’s images.

Things to know

  • Simulation time. The drops are stepped on simulation time: a paused world keeps its drops, and a reset wipes the lens.

  • Under the drops. The camera’s own <noise> and <distortion> are drawn first, so the drops sit on top of them.

  • Cameras. Only type="camera": an RGBD, depth, wide-angle or thermal camera is refused with an error.

  • Cost. The drops are drawn only for frames the camera renders, and only when they changed; a dry lens costs nothing.

  • Image edges. A drop near the edge would show parts of the scene outside the image, which the camera never rendered; they come out a little dark.

  • The behaviour is an estimate. How many drops land per mm/h, their sizes, how fast they slide and how blurred they look are not measured on a real camera. The package README lists every number.
