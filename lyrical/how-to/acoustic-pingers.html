Find an acoustic pinger

RoboNation courses hide acoustic pingers under buoys: a RobotX, RoboBoat or RoboSub vehicle listens with a hydrophone array and works out which buoy pings. gz-maritime simulates the behaviour of that, not the acoustics: pingers ping on a schedule at a frequency, and a receiver on your vehicle reports the direction each ping it hears came from, after the delay of the speed of sound, within a band and a reach, with noise. There is no propagation model: no bending, no reverberation, no waveform.

Every world here runs the acoustics system, silent until a pinger shows up. marina_bay.sdf and marina_bay_lite.sdf have the two pingers of the RobotX 2026 survey task on course B, and acoustic_demo.sdf is a world built to try them.

Run the demo

ros2 launch kai_custom_vehicle acoustic_demo.launch.xml

The open water world with three moored buoys:

Buoy

Where

Pinger

green

(40, 15)

pinger_green, 30 kHz, pinging

red

(40, -15)

pinger_red, 30 kHz, silent

yellow

(-30, 30)

pinger_yellow, 37 kHz, a decoy

Two custom USVs, each with a receiver tuned to 30 kHz. custom_usv starts at the origin and homes on what it hears (pinger_homing.py); boat_b, at (-10, -20), only listens. Neither hears the yellow decoy. homing:=false, listener:=false and gazebo_gui:=false turn the pieces off.

The same demo runs on the RobotX 2026 course, course B of Marina Bay, whose two survey pingers sit about 35 m out from the course’s start point:

ros2 launch kai_custom_vehicle acoustic_demo.launch.xml world:=marina_bay.sdf \
    x:=-15 y:=-8 yaw:=0.55 listener_x:=15 listener_y:=-28 listener_yaw:=0.55

Watch what a boat hears, and the ground truth:

ros2 topic echo /custom_usv/acoustic_receiver
ros2 topic echo /world/default/acoustics/pingers

Switch the course pinger, and custom_usv turns for the red buoy:

ros2 topic pub -1 /world/default/acoustics/pinger_green/set ros_gz_interfaces/msg/ParamVec \
  "{params: [{name: active, value: {type: 1, bool_value: false}}]}"
ros2 topic pub -1 /world/default/acoustics/pinger_red/set ros_gz_interfaces/msg/ParamVec \
  "{params: [{name: active, value: {type: 1, bool_value: true}}]}"

See them in the GUI

Every world docks an Acoustics panel on the right of the Gazebo GUI. It lists the pingers, each with a switch to turn it on or off, and the receivers, each with its last reading and a field to retune it in kHz. It draws, each with its own check box:

  • Reach: where each pinger’s reach meets the water, grey and dashed when the pinger is off, and a lighter dashed ring inside it where the receivers start to lose pings;

  • Pings spreading: a ring per ping, slowed down 50 times by default so the eye can follow it;

  • Bearings heard: a ray from each receiver along the bearing it last reported, from where the receiver was when it heard it, so the ray stays on the pinger while the boat turns; red when the reading was invalid.

The reach and the pings are drawn on the water by default, where a boat’s hydrophone listens. For a vehicle under the water, pick a depth or a receiver’s depth in the panel: the circles are then cut at that depth, or at the receiver’s, following it, which is the slice the vehicle really hears in. A pinger 10 m down with a 20 m reach covers 17 m around it at the surface but the full 20 m at its own depth. Pick spheres, in 3D to see the whole of it: the reach as a wireframe sphere and each ping as a sphere growing out of the pinger, cut at the water. Drawn under the water, they are behind the animated sea: look from an underwater camera, or hide the water.

Colours go with frequency, from orange at 25 kHz to violet at 40 kHz, so the 37 kHz decoy is a different colour from the course pingers. The demo world draws all three; every other world starts with the drawing off. The drawings are the GUI’s only: a camera on a vehicle does not see them.

Put a pinger in a world

kai_gazebo ships model://acoustic_pinger: a spar buoy with the pinger 1.5 m under it, 30 kHz once a second with a 300 m reach. The pinger takes the name the model is included under, which scopes its set topic. Change its settings, or the buoy’s colour, per include:

    <!-- The red buoy: 30 kHz, silent until switched on. -->
    <include>
      <uri>model://acoustic_pinger</uri>
      <name>pinger_red</name>
      <pose>40 -15 0 0 0 0</pose>
      <experimental:params>
          <plugin element_id="gz::sim::maritime::AcousticPinger" action="modify">
            <active>false</active>
          </plugin>
          <visual element_id="link::body" action="modify">
            <material><ambient>0.8 0.1 0.1 1</ambient><diffuse>0.8 0.1 0.1 1</diffuse></material>
          </visual>
          <visual element_id="link::top" action="modify">
            <material><ambient>0.8 0.1 0.1 1</ambient><diffuse>0.8 0.1 0.1 1</diffuse></material>
          </visual>
      </experimental:params>
    </include>

experimental:params needs SDFormat 1.9 or later, and a world that is also read by plain XML tools should declare the prefix on its root, xmlns:experimental="http://sdformat.org/schemas/experimental".

A pinger can also go on any link of your own model, a buoy, a mooring or a vehicle, with the plugin directly:

<plugin filename="gz-maritime-acoustic-pinger-system"
        name="gz::sim::maritime::AcousticPinger">
  <name>my_pinger</name>           <!-- default: the model's name -->
  <link_name>link</link_name>      <!-- default: the canonical link -->
  <pose>0 0 -1.5 0 0 0</pose>      <!-- in the link frame -->
  <frequency>30000</frequency>     <!-- Hz -->
  <period>1</period>               <!-- s between pings -->
  <offset>0</offset>               <!-- s into the period -->
  <reach>300</reach>               <!-- m; 0 is no limit -->
  <active>true</active>
</plugin>

Pingers on one period with different offsets ping in turn, as the RoboNation rules have it. A pinger above the water sends nothing.

Give your vehicle a receiver

The receiver follows the sensor pattern: a frame in the URDF, a tiny link in the model, a <frame_id> with the instance name and a bridge entry. The custom USV hangs its hydrophone on a pole, 0.3 m below the waterline (its base_link is on the waterline), so it stays in the water as the boat rolls:

    <xacro:sensor_link sensor_name="acoustic_receiver" frame="hydrophone_link">
      <sensor name="acoustic_receiver" type="custom" gz:type="acoustic_receiver">
        <frame_id>${name}/hydrophone_link</frame_id>
        <topic>${name}/acoustic_receiver</topic>
        <always_on>true</always_on>
        <gz:acoustic_receiver>
          <frequency>30000</frequency>
          <bandwidth>1000</bandwidth>
          <bearing_noise>
            <noise type="gaussian">
              <mean>0</mean>
              <stddev>0.03</stddev>
            </noise>
          </bearing_noise>
          <elevation_noise>
            <noise type="gaussian">
              <mean>0</mean>
              <stddev>0.03</stddev>
            </noise>
          </elevation_noise>
          <amplitude_noise>
            <noise type="gaussian">
              <mean>0</mean>
              <stddev>1</stddev>
            </noise>
          </amplitude_noise>
          <outlier_probability>0.02</outlier_probability>
          <dropout_start>0.8</dropout_start>
        </gz:acoustic_receiver>
      </sensor>
    </xacro:sensor_link>

It publishes one message per ping it hears, a gz.msgs.Param bridged to ros_gz_interfaces/msg/ParamVec, with these parameters:

Name

What

bearing

rad, counter clockwise from the sensor’s x

elevation

rad, positive up; a pinger below gives a negative value

frequency

Hz of the ping

amplitude

dB relative to 1 m, -20 log10 of the distance, with the receiver’s <amplitude_noise>

valid

false when another ping in band arrived while an earlier one still sounded

range

m, only with <range>true</range>

emit_time

s of simulation time the pinger sent it, only with <emit_time>true</emit_time>

The stamp of a reading is when the ping arrived: when it was sent plus the distance over the speed of sound.

A passive receiver hears a direction, not a distance, but four things give the distance away:

  • range and emit_time. Both are off unless asked for.

  • The stamp. It is the exact arrival, and a pinger pings on a known schedule, so the stamp less the time the ping was sent is the distance over the speed of sound. A real receiver in step with the pinger can do the same, so this one stays.

  • The amplitude. Without noise, 10^(-amplitude / 20) is the distance exactly. Noise around zero only blurs it, since it averages out over a few pings. What hides the range is a bias, which stands for a source level the receiver does not know: a <bias_stddev> in its <amplitude_noise>, with some <stddev>. A receiver with <range> off and neither spread nor bias on its amplitude warns when it loads.

The custom USV’s receiver has 1 dB of amplitude noise and no bias, on purpose. pinger_homing.py stops when the median of the last three amplitudes is louder than -16 dB, which is a distance only with a known source level. A receiver that must hide its range needs the bias and another way to tell it is close, such as the elevation, which steepens as the boat closes on a pinger that hangs deeper than the hydrophone.

Bridge the readings and the retuning topic:

# Acoustic receiver: one message per ping heard, named double parameters
# bearing, elevation (rad, sensor frame), frequency (Hz), amplitude (dB re
# 1 m) and the boolean valid; the stamp is when the ping arrived. Retuned
# with frequency and bandwidth (Hz) on its set topic.
- ros_topic_name: /@name@/acoustic_receiver
  gz_topic_name: /@name@/acoustic_receiver
  ros_type_name: ros_gz_interfaces/msg/ParamVec
  gz_type_name: gz.msgs.Param
  direction: GZ_TO_ROS
- ros_topic_name: /@name@/acoustic_receiver/set
  gz_topic_name: /@name@/acoustic_receiver/set
  ros_type_name: ros_gz_interfaces/msg/ParamVec
  gz_type_name: gz.msgs.Param
  direction: ROS_TO_GZ

A receiver has no <update_rate>: it reports when a ping arrives. A ping is heard when both ends are under the water, the receiver is within the pinger’s reach, the frequency is in the receiver’s band, it comes from inside the receiver’s field of view (<min_elevation> to <max_elevation>, every direction by default), and it isn’t lost to the dropout near the reach. The water is the flat <water_level>, not the waves. The full list of tags is in the gz_acoustics README.

Coming from VRX

VRX’s AcousticPingerPlugin kept the pinger as a position inside the boat’s plugin and published range, bearing and elevation at a fixed rate. Here:

  • the pinger is a model, or a plugin on any link, and moves with it; there can be any number, and every vehicle hears the same ones;

  • a reading comes per ping, with the ping’s frequency, after the travel time;

  • bearing and elevation keep their names and meaning, measured from the sensor’s own frame; range is there only when the receiver asks for it, since a passive receiver cannot measure it;

  • a pinger is switched on or off, and its frequency, period, offset, reach and pulse length changed, through its set topic. It has no position to publish: it moves with its model.
